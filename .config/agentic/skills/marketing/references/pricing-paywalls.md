# Pricing and Paywalls

## First price

On day one there is no price to optimize, only a bet to learn from. Pick, ship, let real buyers react.

- Pick the order of magnitude by who pays. Around $5 to $10 a month for an individual consumer app, around $100 for a small team tool, around $1,000 for business critical software.
- Avoid the ultra-low trap. A $1.99 or $2.99 subscription brings signups that look like traction from people who would never pay a real price, and raising it 5x later is far harder than starting higher.
- Charge something and watch. Paid without hesitation means raise it, nobody paid means you learned it in a week.

## Structure

- Value metric, what the price scales with, has to grow with the value the user gets. Ask "using more of it, do they get more value". Flat price is fine for a simple app.
- Price between the next best alternative, the floor, and the value the user perceives, the ceiling. Competitor prices and your costs are data points, never the basis.
- Tiers, when there are any: an entry tier, a recommended middle tier, a premium tier at 2 to 3 times the middle. Most indie apps need only free plus one paid tier.
- Mobile subscriptions: offer monthly and annual, annual at roughly 2 months free, and a free trial when the value needs a few days to show.

## Raising prices

Signs: nobody flinches, "so cheap" feedback, very high conversion, very low churn, a lot of value shipped since. Raise it for new users first and watch conversion, then move existing users in small waves with months of notice, the reason, and a generous option such as locking the old price on annual. Grandfather as a grace period, not forever.

## Paywalls

Ask after value, never before.

- Show it after the moment the user gets the point, at a real limit, or on a paid feature tap. Never during onboarding, never mid-flow, never again soon after a dismissal, cool down for days.
- Content: a headline about what they get, a preview of the paid feature, the difference between plans with the current one marked, a clear price with monthly and annual, one specific CTA, and an obvious "Not now" or "Continue free".
- Feature lock: what the feature does, why it is paid, a quick path to unlock, a way to continue without.
- Usage limit: the limit reached, what upgrading gives, an alternative action such as deleting an item.
- Trial ending: warnings at 7, 3, and 1 days, what happens at expiry, what they accomplished in the trial.
- After upgrade: instant access, a receipt, and a pointer to the new features.

Never hide the close button, confuse plan selection, guilt-trip, or block a critical flow. Apple and Google review paywalls, a misleading one gets the release rejected.

## Pricing page

Prices in text, never in an image or behind "contact us", so both people and AI assistants can read them. The recommended plan highlighted, a monthly and annual toggle, who each plan is for, an FAQ, and a guarantee if one exists. Schema and AI readability go to `/seo`.

## Measure

Paywall views, tap-through, completed purchases, revenue per user, and churn after upgrade. Test one thing at a time, trigger timing, headline, price presentation, trial length.
