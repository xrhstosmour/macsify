# Landing Page Copy

## Before writing

Know the one action the page asks for, who arrives and from where, what they already know, and the proof available. Take it from the product context file, ask only for what is missing.

Then name the situation in one line with its evidence, it sets how much may change:

- Blank or generic: build the argument from the context, freedom on angle, none on facts.
- A draft with a real voice: keep its argument and words, fix the buried lead, vague lines, and structure. If they write "stuff" and "ship", do not upgrade to "solutions" and "deploy".
- A point edit: change only what was asked and the minimum around it.

## Drafting

- Split the page into units, headline, subhead, CTA, each section, proof lines. Draft two or three options per unit, keep the best, fix its worst flaw, lock it, then read the whole page once.
- Every line passes three questions: can the reader picture it, can it be proven true or false, could a competitor write the same sentence. Fails two or more, rewrite from facts or mark `[NEED: ...]`.
- Most lines are plain and specific. One or two punchy moments per page, usually the headline.
- Before delivering, revert any change that is different without being better.

## Principles

- Clear beats clever, specific beats vague, "weekly report in 15 minutes instead of 4 hours", not "save time".
- Benefits over features, connect feature to what it means for the user.
- The customer's words over the company's.
- One idea per section, each section moves the argument forward.
- Only claims the material supports. A declined claim names the closest supportable version and the evidence that would unlock the original.

## Never write

- Contrast reveals, "it's not X, it's Y", "not just X, but Y". State Y with its reason.
- Negation lists, "no setup, no templates, no waiting". Say what happens. One real absence near the CTA is fine, "no card required".
- A finished claim with more comma clauses piled after it.
- Self-answered questions and colon reveals, "the result? 3x faster".
- Stock phrases, "in today's fast-paced world", "say goodbye to", "unlock the power of", "X, reimagined", and filler words, seamless, robust, powerful, streamline.
- Dashes in headlines, subheads, ads, and short posts. Exclamation marks anywhere.

## Page structure

Above the fold: a headline with the single most important message, a subhead of one or two sentences adding specifics, and a CTA naming what they get, "Start free trial", "Get the app", never "Submit" or "Learn more". Shape the hero as a transformation, today's pain, the better state, the step to get there.

Then as needed: proof, the problem, 3 to 5 benefits, how it works in 3 or 4 steps, objections and FAQ, a final CTA with risk reversal.

Landing page from an ad or a post: one message, one CTA, headline matching the source. Pricing page: make the recommended plan obvious, see `pricing-paywalls.md`.

## Output

Point edit: only the changed lines, one reason each. Full page: copy per section, a one-line rationale on key choices, 2 or 3 headline and CTA alternatives, plus the page title and meta description, leave deeper search work to `/seo`.
