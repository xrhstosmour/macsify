# Deletion request template

Written 2026-11-08 from the GDPR text as known, not yet checked by a lawyer or against current EDPB guidance. A formal request citing the GDPR, for a controller that processes the data of a person in the EU. It is not legal advice. The user fills in every field in angle brackets in their own mail client, the agent never fills in an identifier.

Send it to the controller's privacy or data protection officer address from its privacy policy, keep the sent email as proof of the date.

## Request

Subject: Request under Articles 15, 17 and 21 GDPR, <full name>

To the data protection officer of <company>,

I am writing to exercise my rights as a data subject under the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679.

I believe you process personal data about me. To let you find it, these are the identifiers I believe you hold. Use them only to handle this request:

- Name: <full name>
- Email address: <email>
- Phone number: <phone>
- Other: <username, past city, or profile URL where my data appears>

1. Access, Article 15. Please confirm whether you process my personal data and, if so, give me a copy of it, the purposes of processing, the categories of data, the recipients it was disclosed to, the period it will be stored, and the source it came from, Article 15(1)(g), if you did not collect it from me.

2. Objection, Article 21. I object to the processing of my personal data for direct marketing, including profiling related to it, under Article 21(2). This right is absolute. I also object to any processing based on legitimate interests under Article 21(1), as I see no compelling legitimate grounds that override my interests, rights and freedoms.

3. Erasure, Article 17. Please erase all my personal data without undue delay, under Article 17(1)(a), (c) and (d): the data is not necessary for any purpose it was collected for, I object to the processing under Article 21 and there are no overriding legitimate grounds, and the data has been processed unlawfully, as I never consented and no other lawful basis under Article 6(1) applies.

4. Recipients, Article 19. Please notify every recipient to whom you disclosed my personal data of this erasure, and tell me who they are.

5. Suppression. If you must keep a minimal record so that my data is not collected again, keep only what is needed for that purpose and tell me what it is.

Under Article 12(3) you must respond within one month of receipt. Under Article 12(6) you may ask only for information needed to confirm my identity. The identifiers above should be enough, I will not send a copy of an identity document unless you explain why the identifiers above cannot confirm my identity.

If I do not receive a complete answer within one month, I will lodge a complaint with the supervisory authority, under Article 77.

Please confirm receipt of this request.

<full name>
<date>

## Follow-up after one month

Subject: Follow-up: request under Articles 15, 17 and 21 GDPR of <date sent>, <full name>

To the data protection officer of <company>,

On <date sent> I sent you a request under Articles 15, 17 and 21 GDPR, copied below. The one-month period under Article 12(3) ended on <deadline>, and I have received <no answer, an incomplete answer, a refusal without reasons>.

Please complete the request within 14 days. After that I will lodge a complaint with <supervisory authority> without further notice.

<full name>

## Complaint outline for the supervisory authority

Use the authority's own online form where it has one. Include:

- The controller's name and the address the request went to.
- The date of the request and of the follow-up, with copies.
- What was asked, what was answered, and what is missing.
- Where the data appears, a URL or a screenshot the user took, if any.
- The outcome wanted: erasure, the access copy, and an order to stop processing.

The authority is the one of the user's country of residence, the EDPB site lists them all.

## Shorter version for a search engine delisting

Use the search engine's own form. Name the URLs, say the information is about a private person, out of date or no longer relevant, and has no public interest, citing Article 17 GDPR and the right to be forgotten.
